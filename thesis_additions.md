## ADD-03 — § 02 Tobacco era expansion

**Placement:** REPLACE the current "tobacco era" paragraph.

**The tobacco era (1981–2006).** Cigarette brands were the single largest category of F1 title sponsor for 25 consecutive years. Thirteen tobacco brands held title slots between them — Marlboro, Camel, Rothmans, John Player Special, Mild Seven, Gauloises, Gitanes, Lucky Strike, Benson & Hedges, West, Winfield, Barclay, and Skoal Bandit — sometimes multiple at the same time. In 1999, seven of the eleven teams on the grid had a tobacco brand in their team name — the peak concentration in the dataset.

The dependency went both ways. When the UK Labour government announced a planned ban on tobacco sponsorship after winning the 1997 election, Bernie Ecclestone — F1's chief executive at the time — donated £1 million to the party. Labour subsequently proposed that Formula 1 be granted a special exemption from the EU's tobacco advertising directive, [a reversal that became known as "Berniegate"](https://www.npr.org/1997/11/12/1037747/tobacco-tories). The donation was eventually returned amid public outcry. [The EU directive that ultimately took effect in July 2005](https://www.grandprix.com/features/feature-the-history-of-anti-tobacco-legislation.html) made no exceptions for motor racing.

Title sponsorship in this era was, effectively, a tobacco market — one of the few channels left where cigarette logos could reach a global television audience after national bans had closed the rest off.

## ADD-04 — § 02 Vacuum era expansion

**Placement:** REPLACE the current "vacuum" paragraph.

**The vacuum (2007–2016).** The collapse in tobacco title sponsorship was sharp: from three tobacco title sponsors in 2006 to one in 2007. Ferrari, in partnership with Philip Morris, was the holdout — continuing to display Marlboro-affiliated "barcode" graphics on the car through 2010 in races held in countries where tobacco advertising remained legal. [Public health researchers later argued the barcodes functioned as alibi advertising](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3595501); Ferrari maintained they were part of the team livery.

Telecoms tried to fill the space — AT&T at Williams, Vodafone at McLaren, Orange at Arrows — and most exited within a few years. Industry diversity actually fell during this decade rather than rising; the data shows seven NAICS sectors represented in the 1990s versus only five in the 2010s. Title sponsorship as a structural feature of the sport began declining at the same time, even as overall F1 sponsorship revenue grew. Teams were learning to package one large contract into thirty smaller ones.

## ADD-05 — § 02 Reinvention era expansion

**Placement:** REPLACE the current "reinvention" paragraph.

**The reinvention (2017–2026).** [Liberty Media completed its acquisition of F1 on January 23, 2017, for $4.4 billion](https://www.libertymedia.com/news/detail/305/liberty-media-corporation-completes-acquisition-of-formula-1). The American media company brought a different theory of the sport: less aristocratic European tradition, more global entertainment property. Two years later, _Drive to Survive_ premiered on Netflix. [By 2022, F1 was averaging over one million US viewers per race for the first time](https://www.pressreader.com/france/euronews-english-edition/20220518/282097755309687), with 34% of its global audience under the age of 24.

The post-Liberty era is the first sustained expansion of F1 sponsorship since the tobacco era ended. Three categories now share what tobacco used to hold: software (Oracle, HP, Atlassian, Cognizant), oil and gas (Aramco, Petronas, Orlen) — much of it state-owned — and a wave of crypto and fintech (Visa Cash App, MoneyGram, Stake). Where tobacco was one industry with one motive, the current grid is many industries with overlapping ones. Aramco wants to be seen as a technology company, not just an oil company. Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund wants a foothold in Western luxury sport. Oracle wants its name next to Ferrari and Red Bull because enterprise software is now a brand business. Stake.com — registered in Curaçao, banned from advertising in the UK — wants the legitimacy that comes with appearing in the same camera frame as Lewis Hamilton.

## ADD-06 — § 03 Middle East wave expansion

**Placement:** REPLACE the current "Wave two" paragraph.

**Wave two — the Middle East (post-2018).** Aramco joined Aston Martin as a commercial partner in 2022 and [became sole title partner in 2024](https://www.flashscore.com/news/auto-racing-formula-1-aramco-becomes-aston-martin-s-sole-f1-title-sponsor-for-2024-season/6NiBkUqn), in a five-year deal that coincided with Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund increasing its stake in Aston Martin's road-car parent company to 20.5%. Aramco called the deal a chance to deliver "best-in-class technology" through "advanced fuels and lubricants" — language designed to position a sovereign oil company as a technology partner.

Orlen — Polish, not Middle Eastern, but with similar state-energy logic — joined Alfa Romeo in 2020. Across the same period, Saudi Arabia, Bahrain, Abu Dhabi, and Qatar all hosted races, and sovereign-wealth-adjacent sponsorship followed the calendar. The pattern is structural: as the sport added Gulf races, Gulf money flowed back into the teams.

## ADD-07 — § 04 US growth bullet expansion

**Placement:** REPLACE the current "US growth" bullet.

**US growth.** The most consistent geographic shift across 2020–2026 is the rise of US-headquartered sponsors — from 24% to 41%, a 17-point swing in six years. That tracks directly with Liberty Media's stated strategic priority of expanding F1's American audience. Liberty paid $4.4 billion for the sport in 2017 with explicit shareholder commitments to growing US revenue. _Drive to Survive_ launched on Netflix in 2019; the Miami Grand Prix debuted in 2022; Las Vegas followed in 2023. [By the time Netflix released its fourth season, the show was the most-watched series in 33 countries during its release weeks](https://medium.com/@plumresearch/drive-to-survive-how-netflix-ignited-the-love-for-formula-1-in-the-united-states-c60169b911ec). US sponsorship followed the audience.

## ADD-08 — § 05 Reported crypto narrative

**Placement:** REPLACE the current § 05 prose entirely (the two paragraphs above the chart). Keep the chart and the "FTX collapse" callout box intact.

The cryptocurrency era in Formula 1 lasted thirty-six months and ended at the São Paulo Grand Prix.

In 2020, two crypto brands appeared on F1 cars — both at smaller teams, both at the smallest sponsorship tier. By the start of 2022, that number was thirteen. Nine of the ten teams carried at least one crypto partner. [Mercedes had FTX on the W13's halo, in a deal signed in September 2021](https://decrypt.co/114223/mercedes-f1-suspends-ftx-deal) with a company then valued at $25 billion. Red Bull had Bybit. McLaren had Tezos. Even Ferrari — partnered with Philip Morris for four decades — added the blockchain platform Velas to its livery.

On November 11, 2022, FTX founder Sam Bankman-Fried filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in a Delaware courtroom. The exchange had been valued at $32 billion in January; by November it was insolvent. [Mercedes suspended its partnership the same day](https://www.motorsport.com/f1/news/mercedes-suspends-ftx-sponsor-deal-removes-logos-from-f1-cars/10398017/). "As a first step, we have suspended our partnership agreement with FTX," a team spokesperson said. "This means the company will no longer appear on our race car and other branded assets from this weekend." At the São Paulo Grand Prix two days later, FTX patches had been removed from Hamilton and George Russell's race suits. The corner of the halo where the logo had been was covered in plain black tape. Russell won the race.

The fallout was not contained to Mercedes. By the start of 2023, the crypto count on the grid had dropped from thirteen to nine. [Ferrari and Velas parted ways](https://sportskeeda.com/f1/news-ferrari-joins-mercedes-dropping-crypto-sponsor-nft-market-plummets) as the NFT market collapsed. Alfa Romeo lost three crypto partners in a single off-season.

What's striking is what happened next. Crypto did not exit Formula 1. By 2026, nine crypto sponsors are active on the grid — fewer than the 2022 peak, but the names have almost completely turned over. FTX, Vauld, Velas, Iqoniq, QNTMPAY, and Floki are gone. OKX, Binance, Bybit, Kraken, Gate, and Tezos remain or have arrived. The category survived. The specific gamble each team made on it did not.

In November 2023 — one year after the FTX collapse — [a group of former FTX customers named Mercedes-AMG Petronas as a defendant in a class-action lawsuit](https://www.classaction.org/news/mlb-mercedes-f1-team-media-agencies-hit-with-class-actions-over-ftx-promotions), alleging the team had helped promote a fraudulent exchange and seeking damages from the estimated $11 billion in customer losses. Major League Baseball was named in a parallel suit. "Anyone that mass promotes products [like] FTX's can be sued for any and all resulting damages," lead plaintiff's attorney Adam Moskowitz argued in the filing. The case is ongoing.

## ADD-09 — FTX callout box expansion

**Placement:** REPLACE the text inside the existing FTX callout box. Keep the box element/styling itself.

**FTX collapse — November 2022.** Sam Bankman-Fried's exchange files for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in Delaware after a liquidity crisis driven by Alameda Research's exposure to its own FTT token. Mercedes suspends its FTX deal the same day and removes branding for the São Paulo Grand Prix. The Mercedes deal was the most prominent F1 sponsorship collapse since Leyton House at March in 1991 — when team owner Akira Akagi was arrested in the Fuji Bank fraud scandal, ending the team mid-season. Bankman-Fried was convicted on seven counts of fraud, conspiracy, and money laundering in November 2023 and sentenced to 25 years in federal prison.
